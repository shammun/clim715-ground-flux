Conclusions Four findings — and what they mean for urban climate models
Stability · error structure · mechanism · UHI implication.
1

Stability is structural  [§7 (i)]

FTCS bound is set by the most thermally stiff layer on a stretched grid: 33 s on the asphalt road, 17 s on the concrete deck — below any operational mesoscale step. Implicit treatment of vertical conduction is structurally required.

2

Errors substrate-dependent — CN halves BTCS  [§7 (ii)]

BTCS and CN are unconditionally stable on every run. At Δt = 600 s, BTCS over-amplifies the diurnal G amplitude by 40 / 41 / 19% on asphalt / roof / soil; CN halves these to 21 / 26 / 10%. Peak Ts RMSE: 3.7 K on the roof.

3

Mechanism: operator splitting · κtop sets prefactor  [§7 (iii)+(v)]

Δt-refinement ratios ~10 for both BTCS and CN — first-order in Δt regardless of scheme order. The dominant error is operator splitting between the column and SEB updates. SHAP on a 150-column synthetic ensemble: κtop alone explains R² = 0.92 of error variance; admittance is the residual — consistent with the FTCS-bound parameter setting the BTCS coarse-Δt prefactor.

4

UHI implication: diurnal-range bias  [§7 (iv)]

Daily-mean storage ∫ G dt is preserved across schemes (within ±5%) — the bias is in the diurnal range of the simulated nocturnal UHI, not its mean. At Δt = 60 s BTCS: ~6% nocturnal G inflation on urban surfaces; at Δt = 600 s: 40%.

Limitations Strict-impervious LE = 0 (realistic for new pavement, underestimates real urban latent flux); symmetric synthetic forcing (real cities show asymmetric morning warming, slow evening cooling); independent columns rather than facet-weighted urban canopy; constant U = 3 m s−1 (Newton SEB Jacobian would need to be updated each step under time-varying wind).
Future work Couple this diagnostic framework to an offline run of WRF-SLUCM or WRF-TEB urban canopy schemes with real city substrate composition and flux-tower-derived forcing.
