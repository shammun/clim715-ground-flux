Δt-refinement ratios identify the dominant error as a first-order operator splitting — not the schemes' own temporal accuracy.
RMSE Ts ratio — Δt = 600 s ÷ Δt = 60 s
Why this is decisive

If the per-substep truncation error of each scheme dominated, the Δt-refinement ratio between Δt = 600 s and Δt = 60 s would be:

BTCS (1st-order)~10 CN (2nd-order)~100

Empirically, both schemes show ratios in the range 8.2–10.2 across all three substrates — CN is not behaving like a second-order scheme.

Conclusion: the dominant error is the first-order operator splitting between the column update and the SEB update (run column then update Ts0). CN's α = ½ weighting only halves its prefactor — it cannot remove the splitting. Course Notes #10 §8 warned about this exact failure mode.
